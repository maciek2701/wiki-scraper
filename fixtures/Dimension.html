A dimension, sometimes referred to as a world, is any of several alternate realities or planes of existence in the Pokémon world. While trivial references to other dimensions were made early in the franchise's history, with Pokémon Diamond, Pearl, and Platinum being the first to make use of the idea in its plot. Heavier use of the topic would be explored in Pokémon Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire, Pokémon Sun and Moon, and later, the Pokémon Legends subset of games. Pokémon Legends: Arceus uses the word "world" to describe something coming from another time period.

Different dimensions may hold different entirely unique ecosystems, and by extension, different species of Pokémon and people. Certain Pokémon may have methods of traversing into these different realms, or otherwise have the ability to manipulate them. Often, a source of dimensional travel is used to explain the appearance of wild Pokémon abnormal to what is typically found in the location, such as Hyperspace Holes, Ultra Wormholes, and space-time distortions.

In fiction, a collection of parallel dimensions and worlds that interlink is called a multiverse. The franchise may hold a multiverse depending on the medium and are infrequently implied or mentioned to refer to version differences and game lore. This is referred to by fans as the Pokémon multiverse. Beginning in Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire, there has been heavy implication that each game may be considered its own world.

In the core series games

Dialga's world

Dialga's world is the unnamed dimension in which the Legendary Pokémon, Dialga resides. It has never made any direct appearance in the core series. The only known gateway to this world is atop Mount Coronet, specifically at Spear Pillar/Temple of Sinnoh.

In Pokémon Diamond, Platinum, and Brilliant Diamond, Cyrus uses the Red Chain to summon Dialga from its homeworld at Spear Pillar in the Sinnoh region. In Platinum, the player can encounter, battle, and catch Dialga by returning to Spear Pillar. It'll appear through a blue dimensional rift which leads to its homeworld.

In Pokémon Alpha Sapphire, the player can encounter, battle, and catch Dialga by soaring in the sky of the Hoenn region, it'll appear through a gap in the sky, specifically one that stopped the time of the area surrounding it, which is implied to lead to Dialga's home dimension.

In Pokémon Legends: Arceus, Dialga emerges from a dimensional rift at the Temple of Sinnoh in the Hisui region during Mission 17: "Atop Mount Coronet" or Mission 18: "The Counterpart", depending if the player choses Adaman or Irida respectively, where it'll be battled and caught.

Palkia's world

Palkia's world is the unnamed dimension in which the Legendary Pokémon, Palkia resides. It has never made any direct appearance in the core series. The only known gateway to this world is atop Mount Coronet, specifically at Spear Pillar/Temple of Sinnoh.

In Pokémon Pearl, Platinum, and Shining Pearl, Cyrus uses the Red Chain to summon Palkia from its homeworld at Spear Pillar in the Sinnoh region. In Platinum, the player can encounter, battle, and catch Palkia by returning to Spear Pillar. It'll appear through a pink dimensional rift which leads to its homeworld.

In Pokémon Omega Ruby, the player can encounter, battle, and catch Palkia by soaring in the sky of the Hoenn region, it'll appear through a gap in the sky, specifically one that twisted the space surrounding it, which is implied to lead to Palkia's home dimension.

In Pokémon Legends: Arceus, Palkia emerges from a dimensional rift at the Temple of Sinnoh in the Hisui region during Mission 17: "Atop Mount Coronet" or Mission 18: "The Counterpart" depending if the player choses Irida or Adaman respectively, where it'll be battled and caught.

Distortion World

The Distortion World is the dimension in which the Legendary Pokémon, Giratina resides after it was banished to it by Arceus for its violent nature. Giratina remains in its Origin Forme while in this world. It is a dark place with floor-like sections floating at many different angles. The world disobeys the normal laws of physics, allowing the trainers to walk and surf on walls in any direction. The two known gateways to this world are atop Mount Coronet at Spear Pillar/Temple of Sinnoh and inside Turnback Cave.

In Pokémon Platinum, after Cyrus uses Red Chain to summon both Dialga and Palkia to create his new world at Spear Pillar in the Sinnoh region, Giratina emerges from the Distortion World to stop him from destroying both worlds and drags him into it, trapping him there. After Cyrus is taken away, the player followed both, and with the help of the Lake guardians and Cynthia, explored the area.

In Pokémon Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire, the player can encounter, battle, and catch Giratina by soaring in the sky of the Hoenn region, it'll appear through a gap in the sky, specifically one which a peculiar scenery stretches beyond it, which is heavily implied to lead to the Distortion World.

In Pokémon Legends: Arceus, after Volo is defeated by the player, Giratina emerges from a dimensional rift at the Temple of Sinnoh in the Hisui region during Mission 26: "Seeking the Remaining Plates" to help him, but they're defeated again, forcing it to return to the Distortion World. Later, during Request 91: "On the Trail of Giratina", Giratina reappeared in Turnback Cave from another rift, very weakened after the previous battle, where it can be battled again and caught by the player.

Its animated series counterpart, the Reverse World, is featured in Giratina and the Sky Warrior.

Arceus' realm

Arceus' realm is an unnamed dimension beyond both time and space in which the Mythical Pokémon, Arceus resides. This realm appears as an endless, dark void where nothing exists apart from the Original One itself, in the form of a bright golden light, possibly its true form. Although Arceus frequently appears in the Hall of Origin, it is unclear whether the Hall of Origin is part of Arceus's realm or simply a place located above the Sinnoh/Hisui region. There are no known gateways to this world as apparently it can be only accessed by the Arceus itself.

In Pokémon Legends: Arceus, the player is suddenly brought to Arceus's realm where it welcomes them, introduces itself, and transforms their phone into the Arc Phone before sending them back in time to the Hisui region, with the goal of seeking out all Pokémon.

Ultra Space

Ultra Space is an interdimensional realm similar to space, said to exist between many different worlds via Ultra Wormholes. It is a purplish-colored spatial realm covered with purple crystalline structures, energy spheres, electrified spheres, and many warp holes leading to different worlds. The only Pokémon known to inhabit Ultra Space are Cosmog, its final Evolutions; Solgaleo and Lunala; and Necrozma, which all possess the ability to create Ultra Wormholes. Each of the linked worlds are inhabited by different Pokémon, Ultra Beasts, and even human beings. Professor Mohn had conducted research on Ultra Space and discovering their existence. Currently Professor Burnet in Heahea City studies Ultra Space.

Prior to the events of Pokémon Sun and Moon, Mohn of the Aether Foundation had done research on Ultra Wormholes and Ultra Space, before disappearing, leaving only some papers on Nihilego and Ultra Space, as well as a weakened Cosmog. During the events of the story, Aether President Lusamine forces Nebby, to use its power and open countless Ultra Wormholes all over Alola, allowing Ultra Beasts to invade from their homeworld. While Lusamine and Guzma leave through an Ultra Wormhole themselves, traveling to "Ultra Space". During the game's climax Nebby, now as a SolgaleoS/LunalaM opens another Ultra Wormhole at Poni's altar at Vast Poni Canyon so that Lillie and the player can save Lusamine and Guzma. In the post game, the can travel through a dimensional rift and back through an Ultra Wormhole to an alternate Alola, allowing them catch a new Cosmog.

In Pokémon Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon, Lusamine instead uses Nebby to open a single Ultra Wormhole so she and Guzma can travel to Ultra Space to capture Necrozma. Like in Sun and Moon, Lillie and the player ask Nebby to take them to Ultra Space, however, as Nebby is about to help, a new Ultra Wormhole emerges before it. Lusamine and Guzma are spat out of the warp hole as Necrozma follows, initiating battle with Nebby and absorbs its light, turning into Dusk ManeUS/Dawn WingsUM. After a fight with the player, Necrozma and opens countless more Ultra Wormholes, causing Ultra Beasts to invade. Necrozma flees through another Ultra Wormhole and into Ultra Megalopolis. The player then use the Ultra Recon Squad's LunalaUS/SolgaleoUM to go after it. This method of travel is called Ultra Warp Ride.

In Episode RR, villainous team leaders from parallel worlds where their plans had succeeded appear in Alola through Ultra Wormholes. They're all members of Team Rainbow Rocket and are led by Giovanni. Over the course of the episode, each of the villains are sent back to their homeworlds by Colress except for Giovanni, who after being defeated, leaves on his own to spread his evil on another world.

The Max Lair in Galar's Crown Tundra contains many Pokémon from outside the region. This is due to the result of them passing through Ultra Space from an Ultra Wormhole opened by a Solgaleo and Lunala.

The player can travel to different worlds through Ultra Warp Ride including:

  • Ultra Deep Sea: A dark, glassy cave-like world resembling the bottom of the ocean. It is inhabited by Nihilego.
  • Ultra JungleUS: A jungle-like world with volcanoes that is inhabited by Buzzwole. Many of its greenery resemble flexing body builders.
  • Ultra Desert UM: A white desert-like world with many teal rock-like structures and crystals, similar to buildings. It is inhabited by Pheromosa.
  • Ultra Plant: A dark world full of mountains and in constant thunderstorms. It is inhabited by Xurkitree. Many of its geography resembles wires, and holds electrified rocks.
  • Ultra ForestUS: A Japanese garden-like world full of nature, rocks and sand. It is inhabited by Ultra Forest Kartenvoys and Kartana.
  • Ultra CraterUM: A huge yellowish world full of craters and gases coming out of the ground. It is inhabited by Celesteela.
  • Ultra Ruin: A dystopian city-like location that resembles Hau'oli City in the Alola region. It is nearly uninhabited by human people, with exception of an unnamed suited man. It is home to the Ultra Beast Guzzlord.
  • Ultra Space Wilds: Four different worlds set in the wilderness of the Ultra Space.
    • Cliff World: A reddish mountainous-like world. It is inhabited by many different Pokémon.
    • Water World: An underwater cave-like world. It is inhabited by many different Pokémon.
    • Rocky World: A purplish rock-covered world. It is inhabited by many different Pokémon.
    • Cave World: A dark, blueish cave-like world. It is inhabited by many different Pokémon.
  • Ultra Megalopolis: A light-less, city-like world which had its light stolen long ago by Necrozma. It is inhabited by unfamiliar people (such as the Ultra Recon Squad), Poipole, and Naganadel.[citation needed]

In Pokémon the Series, Ultra Megalopolis has a counterpart inhabited by Poipole and Naganadel. There's also an exclusive, unnamed world which is inhabited by an unknown Ultra Beast.

Hyperspace Lumiose

Hyperspace Lumiose is a dimension instinctively created and sustained by a Darkrai that had been exposed to the Mega Power leaking from Ange. As Darkrai's powers have the ability to manipulate nightmares, after being enhanced by Mega Power, Hyperspace Lumiose was formed from the subconscious thoughts and memories of the people and Pokémon in Lumiose City. In Pokémon Legends: Z-A's Mega Dimension DLC, distortions to hyperspace started appearing across the city, which can be opened by Hoopa through the use of its Hyperspace Holes to allow temporary access into hyperspace. The distortions began to grow over time, which would've led to them eventually swallowing the real Lumiose if Darkrai wasn't stopped.

Many unnatural elements appear in hyperspace, including regional forms, Legendary and Mythical Pokémon that are not typically found in Lumiose or even Kalos. The species of Pokémon that appear here are stronger than what is usual in the real world, reaching hyperspace levels of up to 200. In addition to Pokémon, many berries are unique to hyperspace, which can be used to bake donuts. As Hyperspace Lumiose is made up of the dreams and subconscious of people and Pokémon, many of the entities that appear may be dream-like manifestations,[1] allowing one to interact with those who may have already passed away. Because of this, certain outcomes in hyperspace may manipulated due to a subconscious want.[2] Many Pokémon and objects found in hyperspace are able to leave, sometimes falling through accidentally.

The player can travel to different pockets through Hoopa's hyperspace holes including:

  • Hyperspace wild zone: A pocket of hyperspace based on Lumiose City that holds many wild Pokémon, similar to that of a wild zone.
  • Hyperspace battle zone: A pocket of hyperspace that takes the form of Lumiose City and holds many trainers, similar to a battle zone.
  • Hyperspace Disaster Arena: A pocket of Hyperspace that formed from an Absol's subconscious.[3]
  • Hyperspace Hunting Grounds: A pocket of hyperspace that formed from a Staraptor's subconscious.[3] It resembles the Disaster Arena.
  • Hyperspace Sushi Paradise: A pocket of hyperspace that resembles a sushi restaurant, housing a Rogue Mega Tatsugiri.
  • Hyperspace Second-Sight Arena: A pocket of hyperspace that formed from a Meowstic's subconscious.[3] It resembles the Disaster and Hunting Grounds Arenas.
  • Hyperspace Infernal Arena: A pocket of hyperspace that a Rogue Mega Heatran's appears. It resembles the Disaster, Hunting Grounds, and Second-Sight Arenas.
  • Hyperspace Newmoon Nightmare: A pocket of hyperspace that takes the form of Newmoon Island housing a Rogue Mega Darkrai.
  • Hyperspace Rogue Mega Arena: Pockets of hyperspace that houses several Rogue Mega Pokémon. It seems to be formed from the player's subconcious.
  • Hyperspace Primordial Sea: A pocket of hyperspace formed from a Kyogre's subconscious.[4] It resembles the Cave of Origin as it appears in Pokémon Alpha Sapphire.
  • Hyperspace Desolate Land: A pocket of hyperspace formed from a Groudon's subconscious.[5] It resembles the Cave of Origin as it appears in Pokémon Omega Ruby.
  • Hyperspace Sky Pillar: A pocket of hyperspace that takes the form of Sky Pillar from Pokémon Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire. It was formed from the subconcious of Ansha and the other members of Team MZ in attempt to summon a Rayquaza. Other areas simply named "Hyperspace Lumiose" take on a similar appearance.

Ghost World

The Ghost World or Spirit World is a location that is home to many Ghost type Pokémon and human ghosts and also implied to be the afterlife of the Pokémon universe. It has never made any direct appearance in the core series. It is known to emit electrical or radio waves. It has never appeared in the games, but is mentioned in all of Dusknoir's Pokédex entries and in the Japanese name of the Reaper Cloth (れいかいのぬの Spirit World Cloth), confirming its existence and a strong relation with Dusknoir.

In the side series games

Pokémon Dream World

Pokémon Dream World

The Pokémon Dream World is an alternate dimension that can acessed by Pokémon when they fall asleep, supposedly connected to the Interdream Zone, which is said to be located between dreams and reality. The Dream World is a paradise-like dimension full of plants, always sunny, and composed of many different floating islands. Many Pokémon can be found in the Dream World.

The Dream World is the main research subject of Fennel.

Pokémon Dream Radar

AD

Interdream Zone

The Interdream Zone is an alternate dimension located between dreams and reality. It takes many different appearances, but always possesses Dream Orbs and Dream Clouds floating around in the air. It is inhabited by several different Pokémon, most notably the Therian Formes of Tornadus, Thundurus, and Landorus, who are said to have been born there.

In Pokémon Dream Radar, it is explored and researched by Professor Burnet and her unnamed assistant, catching as many Pokémon in it they can.

In Pokémon Sun, Moon, Ultra Sun, and Ultra Moon, it is briefly mentioned by a Scientist at the Dimensional Research Lab in Heahea City, which is led by Professor Burnet, who now researches dimensions other than it in Alola, such as Ultra Space.

In the spin-off games

Pokémon Mystery Dungeon series

Voidlands

The Voidlands is a realm that appears in Pokémon Super Mystery Dungeon. It is where the spirits of Pokémon who are turned to stone by Dark Matter are sent to. Beings known as Void Shadows inhabit this dimension.

In animation

Pokémon the Series

Unown Dimension

While researching ruins with depictions of the Unown, Spencer Hale was transported into this dimension. His daughter, Molly, inherited a puzzle box found there that gave her contact with the Unown, which granted her wishes. Such was the strength of the world she created, that an opening to the dimension appeared in the entrance hall of her mansion. During this time, the Unown gained powerful control over Greenfield, but all contact with the dimension ended after Molly wished for things to go back to the way they had been before. A portal to the dimension was opened above Mt. Silver some time later, and an Unown fell out, but it had no trouble returning when it was in good health. The Unown Dimension also became the battleground of Dialga and Palkia, before their battle moved to Alamos Town.

Travel between the Unown Dimension and the real Pokémon world has been shown to be possible through a puzzle box and a portal. The direct connection between ancient sites like the Ruins of Alph and Solaceon Ruins and their large concentrations of Unown remains a curiosity amongst Scientists.

Ghost World

In Pokémon the Series: Diamond and Pearl, an entrance to the Ghost World appeared in Ghoul Daze!, where the Pokémon Summer Academy staff accidentally opened a portal to it when renovating the Summit Ruins. A ghost girl and Dusknoir were both let out, with the Dusknoir trying to take the ghost girl back to the Ghost World, while she was trying to abduct people and take them to the Ghost World. Another entrance appeared in the Black & White series episode Scare at the Litwick Mansion!, where the Litwick in Litwick Mansion tried to lead Ash and his friends there while draining their life energy.

Dialga's world

This dimension was mentioned in Arceus and the Jewel of Life. It is the home of Dialga.

Dialga and Palkia's worlds are usually far away from each other. During the events of The Rise of Darkrai, the two worlds began to converge. Due to shock waves caused by Arceus awakening from its deep slumber, the two worlds clashed, resulting in a battle between Dialga and Palkia, because each Pokémon thought that the other was trespassing onto its territory. The battle subsided a short time before Arceus woke up.

The Time-Space Axis device in Michina Town is able to indicate the condition of every minute change in the time-space continuum, serving to mirror the movements of the time-space dimensions and thus monitoring the likelihood of potential cataclysm.

This world is briefly shown during the opening scene and end credits of Arceus and the Jewel of Life, as well as the opening scene of Hoopa and the Clash of Ages.

Palkia's world

This dimension was mentioned in Arceus and the Jewel of Life. It is the home of Palkia.

Reverse World

This dimension appears in Giratina and the Sky Warrior as where the Legendary Pokémon Giratina inhabits. It is capable of opening portals there through reflections such as in glasses or lakes to come to the Pokémon world.

This world is also shown briefly during the opening scene and end credits of Arceus and the Jewel of Life.

Arceus' world

Arceus was said to have its own dimension in Arceus and the Jewel of Life. It slumbered there for hundreds of years after being "betrayed" by Damos, who refused to give the Jewel of Life back to it and attacked it.

This world is also shown briefly during the end credits of Hoopa and the Clash of Ages.

Lake guardians' dimensions

In Pokémon the Series, the lake guardians are said to each inhabit their own dimension linked to the three lakes of Sinnoh: Uxie lives in Lake Acuity's, Mesprit lives in Lake Verity's and Azelf in Lake Valor's.

These dimensions are never seen in the series.

Ultra Space

Ultra Space is an alternate space said to exist between many different dimensions, including some exclusive to the animated series.

  • Ultra Deep Sea: A purplish blue space full of floating crystal islands and structures. It is only inhabited by Nihilego.
  • Poipole's world: A flowery, mountainous world with rocky nests where Poipole and Naganadel (including Ash's) live in.
  • Ultra Ruin: An alternate version of Alola that was ruined by a Guzzlord. It is inhabited by many different Pokémon, including a Zeraora and many humans including Dia and the Ultra Rangers.
  • Unidentified world: A reddish crystallized world. It is inhabited by an unseen Ultra Beast.
  • Ultra Deep Sea in the Sun & Moon series
  • An unnamed world in the Sun & Moon series
  • Ultra Ruin in the Sun & Moon series
  • An unnamed world in Ultra Space in the Sun & Moon series

Other dimensions

Ash and his friends were transported into the Togepi Paradise, a dimension connected by several gateways throughout the Mirage Kingdom, in A Togepi Mirage!.

Deoxys's unnamed world appeared in Pokémon Ranger - Deoxys' Crisis! Part 1 and Part 2.

Ash and his friends visited the Mirror World, an alternate dimension with strong connections to the Reflection Cave, in The Cave of Mirrors!.

In the short film Pikachu, What’s This Key?, Pikachu and his Pokémon friends travel to numerous dimensions:

  • Klefki's world, a realm where Klefki keeps a collection of keys that unlock portals to other dimensions.
  • A dimension with bouncy clouds and pillars of stacked boulders, inhabited by Victini.
  • A dimension full of ice and snow, home to multiple Pokémon, including Jirachi.
  • A watery dimension with floating spheres of water, home to Manaphy (among other Pokémon).
  • A red-and-black dimension, home to Darkrai.

In The Gates of Warp! and Showdown at the Gates of Warp!, Ash and his friends visited an alternate world version of Sinnoh through gateways opened by Palkia.

In the manga

Pokémon Adventures

Sinjoh Ruins

In All About Arceus VI, Pryce revealed that in this canon, the Sinjoh Ruins are located in a pocket dimension connecting Johto and Sinnoh, which is why they can only be accessed by those accompanied by Arceus. While trapped within the Ilex Forest shrine, Pryce had foreseen a future where a battle between Dialga, Palkia, and Giratina would destroy the ruins and the two regions they were connected to with them. The ruins were eventually saved when Gold managed to restore Arceus's lost faith in humanity and make it stop the battle.

Pokémon Dream World

In Pink Slip, Fennel revealed that the Dragon Stones are connected to the Pokémon Dream World. In Abyssal Ruins, N and White were sucked into the Dream World, where they found Black, who had wound up there after being absorbed into the Light Stone two years earlier. In the next chapter, all three of them escaped from the Dream World. In this realm, they were able to hear Reshiram's voice talking to them.
